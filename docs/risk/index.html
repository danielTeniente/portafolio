02 / SAME MODEL, DIFFERENT CONSEQUENCES

What are you committing to?

Imagine an operational workload with a fixed capacity per hour. The decision rule accepts work only when forecast × exposure ≤ capacity; otherwise it declines that hour. A breach occurs when accepted work actually exceeds capacity.

Accepted commitments
Capacity breaches
Largest accepted overloadLoad above capacity · consequence units

Capacity resets every hour. This is not cumulative loss, bankruptcy, or a financial return model. Declining work is assumed feasible but sacrifices activity; associated costs and revenues are not modeled. Lower exposure reduces both workload and activity. Decisions use only forecasts; actual outcomes are used afterward to score them. A point-forecast gate is deliberately simple, not a recommended safety policy.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE TAIL

Same mean. Different extremes.

The stable worlds use positive innovations with mean 1. One uses Gamma noise (shape 4, scale 0.25); the other uses Pareto noise (survival exponent α = 1.5, minimum 1/3). The same seasonal signal multiplies them. Their means match; their variances do not.

Theoretical survival curves for the innovation multiplier: P(Z > z). Both axes are logarithmic. The displayed range is not a truncation of simulated observations. Here α is the survival exponent; the Pareto density exponent is α + 1.

Light tail

Gamma innovations have finite mean and variance (0.25). Their extreme-event probabilities fall rapidly.

Power-law tail

P(Z > z) = (xₘ / z)α for z ≥ xₘ. With α = 1.5, the mean exists, but population variance is infinite. Finite samples still produce finite RMSE values.

What a plot cannot prove

A straight-looking empirical log–log plot does not establish a power law. Real data needs threshold selection, fit diagnostics and comparison with alternatives such as lognormal or truncated tails.

“Stable regime” refers to unchanged seasonal structure and innovation law. The raw series is seasonal, not strictly stationary in its unconditional mean. Infinite variance is a property of the idealized unbounded Pareto law—not evidence that physical loads can be infinite.

05 / BLACK SWANS & THE LIMITS OF THE EXPERIMENT

Simulating a tail is not predicting a black swan.

These extremes belong to distributions we explicitly chose. They are modeled tail events. A predeclared regime change is also a scenario, not proof that an unknown future shock is predictable.

The practical lesson I take from black-swan thinking is to question what lies outside the model, inspect how consequences grow with exposure, and preserve the ability to stop, adapt or absorb a miss.

Nonstationarity does not guarantee catastrophe. It can invalidate learned relationships, while even an unchanged process can contain severe tail risk. Whether a miss becomes catastrophic depends on the system’s constraints and decision design.

06 / AUDIT THE EXPERIMENT

Transparent by construction.

Data before models

Fixed seeds 42, 7 and 123. Fixed generators, no hand-placed outlier, and no rejection of benign runs. The stable light-tail world is the control. The shift is specified at the midpoint before fitting, not placed at a convenient model failure.

Time boundaries

600 training target days; four expanding validation folds with 90 days each; 150 test days. Training endpoints: 240, 330, 420 and 510 days. Two candidates per model. Final models fit all 600 training days and remain fixed; previous-day observations update daily.

Information limits

The predictors receive only the prior 24 loads. No future innovations, true seasonal signal or regime indicator. Independent shocks are intentionally not forecastable from past observations; this is a limitation of the information set, not a verdict on every forecasting method.

Fair interpretation

These scenarios were chosen to study tail risk, not to represent the prevalence of real-world failures. Four models and a small tuning budget are not a universal comparison. Success in the control is reported alongside misses. Tail-aware forecasts and adaptive retraining are future extensions, not evaluated claims.

Exact model architectures and training settings

All fitted estimators use seed 42, two worker threads where available, and nonnegative output clipping. Only Direct AR scales features; its scaler is fitted within each fold. Model seeds stay fixed while data seeds vary. Other defaults follow the repository’s pinned libraries.

Reproduce and inspect

Run python src/risk_benchmark.py with the repository dependencies. This regenerates docs/risk/data.js and results/risk_report.json. Changing a control here selects a saved run or recalculates a decision—it does not retrain the models.
